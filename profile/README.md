# E-CHANGE

## Boosting Epidemiological Methods to Assess Human Health Risks in a CHANGing ClimatE

**E-CHANGE** is a research project led by the **University of Turin** that aims to advance epidemiological methods for assessing the effects of climate change and climate-related environmental exposures on human health.

The project integrates expertise from epidemiology, biostatistics, climate science, environmental health, molecular epidemiology, and public health.

## Research objectives

E-CHANGE investigates the health effects of climate-related exposures across different temporal scales:

- **Short-term effects**, including the acute health impacts of high temperatures, heatwaves, wildfires, and other extreme events;
- **Medium-term effects**, including climate-sensitive infectious diseases and vector-borne diseases;
- **Long-term effects**, including the influence of climate-related exposures during pregnancy, childhood, and other potentially sensitive periods;
- **Climate-change attribution**, by comparing observed or factual climatic conditions with counterfactual scenarios representing a climate without, or with reduced, anthropogenic influence.

## Work packages

### WP1 — Climate and health data integration

Identification, processing, harmonisation, and integration of climate, environmental, socioeconomic, and health data across different spatial and temporal scales.

### WP2 — Epidemiological analyses

Development and application of epidemiological and biostatistical methods to estimate the short-, medium-, and long-term health effects of climate-related exposures.

### WP3 — Climate-change health attribution

Development of factual and counterfactual climate scenarios and estimation of the health burden attributable to anthropogenic climate change.

### WP4 — Communication, dissemination, and exploitation

Dissemination of research protocols, methods, results, data documentation, and reproducible analysis resources to scientific, institutional, and public audiences.

## Repository contents

The repositories maintained by the E-CHANGE project will include:

- research protocols;
- data dictionaries and metadata;
- climate and environmental data-processing workflows;
- statistical analysis code;
- documentation supporting scientific publications;
- dissemination and training materials.
